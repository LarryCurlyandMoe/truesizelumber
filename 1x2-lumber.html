What size is a 1×2, really?

A 1×2 is sold and labeled as 1×2, but it actually measures ¾" × 1½". The difference comes from milling and drying — the board starts closer to its named size, then shrinks as it's surfaced smooth and dried to standard moisture content.

What a 1×2 is actually used for

Furring strips, lattice, garden stakes, shims

Good to know before you buy

1x2 is thin and prone to cupping — sight down the length of the board in the aisle before buying, and expect more waste per board than thicker stock.

Working with 1×2 in the field

Because there's so little material in a 1x2 to begin with, defects that would barely register in a wider board — a knot near the edge, a shallow check, a slight twist — can compromise the whole piece. Most yards sell 1x2 as unselected 'utility' or 'standard' grade rather than appearance-graded stock, so expect more knots and surface checking than you'd see in 1x4 or wider from the same rack. Sight down both the face and the edge of every board, not just one side — a board that looks flat from the face can still have edge warp that only shows up once it's installed. Because waste from trimming out bad sections is proportionally higher on narrow stock, budget 10-15% extra length beyond your exact cut list, not the 5% rule of thumb that works fine for wider boards.
